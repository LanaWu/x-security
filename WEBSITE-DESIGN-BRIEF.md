# X-Security — Website Information Architecture & UI/UX Design Brief

## Product direction

X-Security is presented as an integrated security and intelligence company (PRD Model B). A client begins with a risk or problem; X-Security assesses the context, designs a proportionate response and connects the relevant specialist capabilities. Training and professional conduct support every division.

The site serves corporate and government buyers, professional firms, event organizers, individuals seeking protection or investigation services, and prospective personnel. It must communicate discretion and competence without militaristic posturing.

## Brand and visual system

### Brand recommendation

- **Palette:** midnight slate #101B22 anchors the site and signals stability; deep blue-green #20343A and muted teal #4C8683 communicate calm expertise; warm off-white #F4F5F2 keeps long-form pages approachable; restrained lime #C8ED68 highlights actions and active status. Lime is an accent, never a large body-text color. Maintain WCAG AA contrast for text and controls.
- **Typography:** Manrope for headings and identity, DM Sans for body, UI and labels. Desktop H1 56–78 px, interior H1 42–66 px, H2 34–52 px, cards 14–19 px, body 12–16 px, eyebrow labels 8–10 px with tracking. Use fluid sizing and keep mobile body copy readable.
- **Logo direction:** a restrained geometric X formed from two intersecting sight lines or paths, optionally held in a compact octagonal frame. It suggests connected disciplines, direction and focus—not weapons, shields, eagles, or a threatening silhouette. The implementation uses a typographic X placeholder pending a designed and legally cleared mark.
- **Tone:** calm, specific, measured and respectful. State what a service does and how it is scoped. Prefer “authorized assessment,” “professional protection,” “confidential inquiry,” and “proportionate response.” Avoid guarantees, fear-driven copy, “elite” without evidence, combat language and unverified performance claims.

### UI rules

- Editorial layouts with clear whitespace, strong grid alignment, short labels and visible page hierarchy.
- Dark, restrained hero sections; light reading surfaces for detailed content.
- Primary action: **Request a consultation**. Secondary: **Explore services**. Service-page CTA: **Discuss this service**.
- Use line icons and abstract diagrams consistently; avoid tactical stock photography and identifiable operational details.
- Real statistics, testimonials, client logos and certifications appear only after verification and approval.
- Do not imply a live monitoring center or real-time threat intelligence unless operated. The hero illustration is an abstract brand graphic, not an operational dashboard.

## Page specifications and wireframes

### Home

**Goal:** communicate integrated positioning in one screen and drive service exploration or consultation.  
**Messaging:** “Integrated security. Built on intelligence.” Training, people, investigation, cyber capability and risk thinking work together.  
**Hierarchy/wireframe:** dark header → two-column hero (eyebrow, H1, value proposition and two CTAs | abstract connected-risk graphic) → operating principles strip → four service cards → assess/design/protect methodology → training pathway → credibility/standards → consultation close → full footer.  
**Visuals:** custom abstract network/risk graphic; original team and training photography can replace it after approval.  
**Conversion:** consultation CTAs in header, hero and page close; service cards route to dedicated pages.

### About X-Security

**Goal:** explain purpose and establish a credible operating philosophy.  
**Messaging:** mission, vision, integrated capability, training-first culture and professionalism.  
**Hierarchy:** page title → mission and vision split layout → four principles → leadership profiles after supplied and approved → standards and compliance CTA.  
**Visuals:** approved leadership portraits, authentic team image, simple capabilities diagram.  
**Conversion:** links to compliance, services, careers and consultation.

### Services overview

**Goal:** help visitors identify the right division, including when the need is not precisely scoped.  
**Messaging:** begin with the risk; combine specialties when useful.  
**Hierarchy:** page introduction → four numbered service cards, each with client need, brief outcome and link → engagement process → contact CTA.  
**Visuals:** four consistent service icons or restrained documentary images.  
**Conversion:** cards and a general consultation action.

### Division pages (Private Investigation, Cybersecurity & Technology, Executive & Venue Protection, Protective Intelligence & Risk)

**Goal:** convert interest in a defined need into a well-scoped conversation.  
**Messaging:** page-specific hero → problem statement → X-Security solution → explicit service scope → process → confidentiality/safety/authorization signals → client fit → approved proof or clearly labeled illustrative scenario → related capabilities → FAQ → service inquiry CTA.  
**Layout:** shared template: Hero / Challenge and approach / Capabilities / How work proceeds / Trust and proof / Related service links / FAQ / CTA.  
**Visuals:** investigations—research/reporting; cyber—abstract architecture, no terminal “hacker” clichés; protection—professional and discreet people; risk—maps or non-sensitive assessment diagrams.  
**Conversion:** “Discuss an investigation,” “Discuss cybersecurity,” “Plan event or personal protection,” or “Request a risk assessment.”

### Training Programs

**Goal:** describe the training advantage and make the pathway legible to recruits and organizational buyers.  
**Messaging:** shared professional foundation, objective readiness assessment, role-specific specialization, supervised deployment and continuous development. Competencies are role-based, not gender-assumed.  
**Hierarchy:** training promise → five-step pathway → common curriculum → specialist pathways → corporate training information → CTA.  
**Visuals:** original training photography, pathway diagram, curriculum icons. Avoid unsafe or weapon-centric scenes as the primary brand image.  
**Conversion:** training inquiry and Careers link.

### Careers

**Goal:** attract candidates who fit the conduct and learning culture.  
**Messaging:** integrity, discipline, self-control, communication and learning matter; physical and technical expectations depend on role.  
**Hierarchy:** recruitment message → candidate profile → career pathways → recruitment steps → approved open roles → application instructions.  
**Visuals:** genuine team portraits and role icons.  
**Conversion:** apply/register interest. Publish openings only when authorized.

### Contact & Inquiry

**Goal:** qualify a lead without inviting disclosure of sensitive information.  
**Messaging:** start with a high-level description; a specialist will help define a next step.  
**Hierarchy:** confidentiality note and confirmed office details → form with service, client type, timeframe, name, email, optional organization and short description → privacy explanation and submission confirmation.  
**Visuals:** clean form panel, clear progress/error feedback.  
**Conversion:** inquiry submission and confirmed phone/email once supplied. Current form includes service, name, email, organization, timeframe and description, with server validation, CSRF token and honeypot. It is a local prototype: no email, CRM or durable storage integration is configured.

### Certifications & Compliance

**Goal:** substantiate trust with records buyers can verify.  
**Messaging:** lawful and authorized work, confidentiality, privacy, safety, current and applicable credentials.  
**Hierarchy:** operating commitments → verified credential register (issuer, scope, jurisdiction and expiry) → insurance and privacy commitments → point of contact.  
**Visuals:** only authentic credential marks and documentation. Prototype states records must be supplied and verified.

### Client Success / Case Studies

**Goal:** show how X-Security’s integrated model solves a client problem.  
**Messaging:** challenge → approach → measurable result, with client permission and confidentiality protections.  
**Hierarchy:** case cards → detailed case pages when available → related capabilities → CTA.  
**Visuals:** anonymized diagrams and approved client imagery. Current cards are expressly marked illustrative scenarios, not completed engagements.

### Insights & Resources

**Goal:** demonstrate expertise and support consideration over time.  
**Messaging:** accurate, practical guidance in corporate security, cyber awareness, investigations, event planning and travel risk.  
**Hierarchy:** curated topics → dated articles with named reviewer → related service CTA.  
**Visuals:** explanatory charts and diagrams. Publish only content with an assigned owner and review date.

### FAQ

**Goal:** answer practical objections and reduce inquiry friction.  
**Hierarchy:** accessible accordion list, organized around coverage, engagement, service combination, cyber authorization, confidentiality, training and credentials.  
**Conversion:** contact CTA after questions.

## Interaction, accessibility and responsive behavior

- Multi-page navigation with desktop dropdowns, keyboard focus support and a mobile collapsible menu. Every major division has a dedicated URL and browser history entry.
- Inquiry form fields use labels, server-side validation, clear errors, CSRF protection and a honeypot. Do not request sensitive operational details or attachments. Production must add a privacy notice, spam controls, secure transport, secure monitored destination and retention rules.
- Potential phase-two service configurator may ask only broad need, organization size, urgency and service interest. It must not expose sensitive assessment outputs or collect exploitable security posture.
- Target WCAG 2.2 AA: semantic landmarks, ordered headings, keyboard operation, visible focus, text contrast, descriptive alt text, accessible validation, touch targets, captions/transcripts, reduced-motion support. Do not encode meaning by color alone.
- Mobile-first priorities: call/contact, services, trust, training; single-column forms and cards; sticky consultation action; reachable menu and minimum 44 px interactive targets.
- No client names, logos, metrics, testimonials, badges or case outcomes without written approval and evidence. Avoid collecting more form data than follow-up requires.

## User journeys

**Exploring services:** Home → service overview → relevant division → related capability / FAQ / case study → consultation. Keep breadcrumbs, related links and a consistent next-step CTA visible.  
**Ready to inquire:** any page → persistent CTA → contact page → choose service and timeframe → submit a high-level brief → success message sets expectations.  
**Prospective candidate:** Home/About → Training → Careers → role pathway and requirements → application route.  
**Corporate risk buyer:** Home → Risk page or Services → assess/design/protect process → compliance / case proof → inquiry.

## Developer handoff

### Current implementation

- index.php is a lightweight PHP front controller and page renderer. Route via ?page=...; page content is in a registry, with shared navigation/footer and reusable service cards.
- assets/css/site.css holds colors, type, component styling, responsive layouts and reduced-motion handling.
- assets/js/site.js manages the mobile menu and Escape-key close behavior.
- No framework scaffold or Composer dependencies were present at repository inspection. This implementation uses native PHP to remain runnable in the supplied empty repo. If Laravel is a hard delivery constraint, migrate the page registry into Laravel routes/controllers/Blade templates before launch.
- Local server command: php -S 127.0.0.1:8000; open / and append ?page=services, ?page=contact, etc. Requires PHP 8.1+ recommended.

### Assets to commission or provide

1. Vector logo and horizontal/stacked variants, monochrome and favicon.
2. Approved leadership headshots and short verified biographies.
3. Original images for investigation, cyber, protection, training, corporate collaboration and venues; usage permissions and alt-text descriptions.
4. Optional 30–60 second brand film, captions and transcript.
5. Service line icons, training pathway, assess/design/protect graphic, risk matrix and approved case-study diagrams.
6. Verified registration/licensing/certification/insurance details and approved testimonial text.
7. Confirmed office location, monitored business email, telephone and supported service geographies.

### Production integrations and launch content

- Connect contact submission to an approved secure email or CRM workflow with access control, retention and spam controls.
- Add privacy policy, cookie consent if applicable, contact response time and jurisdictional notices.
- Configure HTTPS, security headers, web application firewall, MFA for CMS/admin accounts, backups and dependency update process.
- Replace illustrative cases and placeholder office/leadership/credential notes with authorized facts; do not publish empty proof claims.
- If article publishing, hiring or case-study volume grows, migrate page content to a CMS while preserving dedicated routes and review workflows.
- Add analytics events for service views, CTA use, form completion and careers inquiry only after privacy configuration.

### Acceptance checklist

- Dedicated working route for every sitemap page; correct active navigation and page title.
- Responsive behavior at phone, tablet and desktop widths.
- Keyboard and screen reader navigation through menu, page content, form and FAQ.
- Contact form rejects missing/invalid required fields and invalid/expired CSRF tokens; success state explains prototype limitations.
- No unsupported client, certification, performance or staffing claims.
- Images have useful alt text, videos have captions/transcripts and color contrast meets WCAG 2.2 AA.
